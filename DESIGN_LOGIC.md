# Fed Up — Design Logic (why everything is where it is)

> **The designer's rule** (`STORY_BIBLE.md`, "Design logic", 23 Oct 2026): every decision and every object needs a reason. For each thing, ask:
> - Where is the player coming from, and where do they go next?
> - What can they see from there?
> - What does it say about him or the house?
> - What later moment does it set up?
>
> A thing with no reason here gets moved until it has one. **Update this file before building anything new.**

## The evening route (Phase 1)

**Porch → front door → entrance chores → kitchen (toast) → bathroom next door (bath) → back through the living room (clean-up) → couch (TV) → the phone rings in the hallway behind you → the cat comes down that corridor → back to the couch to fall asleep (gate 3).**

- **The first walk is a preview.** The kitchen is visible across the open-plan living room from the front door. The first real task (toast) makes you walk past the TV, the couch and the mess, so you've seen everything before you're asked to deal with it.
- **The night goes deeper, then comes back.** Each task is further from the front door than the last (entrance → kitchen → bathroom at the far east). Then the route turns back west to the living room. You return to the couch, the same place you'll fall asleep.
- **Nothing makes you backtrack without a reason.** The bathroom is next door to the kitchen. The mess lies between the bathroom and the couch.
- **The night pulls you out of rest.** The one time you sit down (TV), the boss's call drags you off the couch into the dark hallway. That hallway is where the cat appears next.

## Falling asleep and Phase 2: the wake-up (Gate 3)

**Route:** couch (lie down) → *3:07 AM* → wake on the couch → **the long hallway** (footsteps behind you) → **downstairs bathroom** (splash water; the mirror) → leave, and **the bathroom door slams behind you** → back to the living room.

**The idea (from the Cry of Fear study):** Phase 2 is the *sick version of the same rooms*. Same house, most of the light taken away, fog and sound added. You've walked this exact route in Phase 1 (toast, bath, phone, cat), so you know it, and now every familiar spot turns on you.

**The idea (from `HORROR_RESEARCH.md`):**
- **The appliances you used in the evening turn on you** (the uncanny).
- **The calm items sit right after the hard stretches** (the sweet spot).

## How the player knows where to go (Gate 5 onwards)

Show-don't-tell is for **foreshadowing**, not for **finding your way**. Getting lost isn't scary, it's just annoying. So every "go somewhere new" has three layers, from loudest to quietest:

1. **The objective line** at the top of the screen says the goal in his words ("Charge your phone. The charger's by the bed, upstairs.").
2. **His voice** gives the reason, in character (*"4%. The charger's upstairs. Of course it is."*).
3. **The house pulls you there.** A light left on, a sound from that direction.

**Going upstairs:**
- **Phase 2, the charger (5a):** the torch starts dying, flickering and dimming. The landing light at the top of the stairs is the one light upstairs that's on. This trip teaches you the way to his bed before the final sleep needs it.
- **Phase 3, the tea (5b):** when the ingredients vanish, a tin drops and rolls across the ceiling above the kitchen, because the Storeroom is directly above it. He says, flatly, *"…That came from upstairs."*
- **The final sleep (5a):** "Go to bed". You've already been to the bed for the charger, so you know the way.
- **The secret ending:** nothing points you upstairs before your first chore. That's what makes it a secret.

## How this is checked

The Play Mode tests check the reasons that can be measured:
- you can't see the phone from the couch, and you can walk to it
- from the phone you have a clear view to where the cat appears
- the couch faces the TV, and nothing blocks the screen
- the Locked Room can't be entered
- clutter never hides behind furniture (the builder needs open floor around each item)
