# Fed Up — Design Logic (why everything is where it is)

> **The designer's rule** (`STORY_BIBLE.md`, "Design logic", 23 Oct 2026): every decision and every object needs a reason. For each thing, ask:
> - Where is the player coming from, and where do they go next?
> - What can they see from there?
> - What does it say about him or the house?
> - What later moment does it set up?
>
> A thing with no reason here gets moved until it has one. **Update this file before building anything new.**

## The evening route (Phase 1)

**Porch → front door → entrance chores → kitchen (toast) → bathroom next door (bath) → back through the living room (clean-up) → couch (TV) → the phone rings in the hallway behind you → the cat comes down that corridor → back to the couch to fall asleep (gate 3).**

- **The first walk is a preview.** The kitchen is visible across the open-plan living room from the front door. The first real task (toast) makes you walk past the TV, the couch and the mess, so you've seen everything before you're asked to deal with it.
- **The night goes deeper, then comes back.** Each task is further from the front door than the last (entrance → kitchen → bathroom at the far east). Then the route turns back west to the living room. You return to the couch, the same place you'll fall asleep.
- **Nothing makes you backtrack without a reason.** The bathroom is next door to the kitchen. The mess lies between the bathroom and the couch.
- **The night pulls you out of rest.** The one time you sit down (TV), the boss's call drags you off the couch into the dark hallway. That hallway is where the cat appears next.

## Entrance (west porch)

| # | Thing | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Porch start** | Designer ruling: the west porch is the main entrance. You start outside at night, under the factory smog, so the house is the only light. |
| 2 | **Front door** | The doorway cut into the north end of the big west window. It opens onto the living room and the kitchen beyond, so the whole evening is in view from the threshold. |
| 3 | **Light switch** | On the wall beside the door, at hand height. It's the first thing anyone touches coming home in the dark. |
| 4 | **Coat hook + shoe rack** | North of the door, where your right hand is as you step in: jacket, then shoes. Coming home is a ritual. The jacket that falls later works because you saw it hung up. |
| 5 | **Side table + bag** | South of the door, so you turn around in the doorway to empty the bag. That turn is the camera move for the file discovery. GDD: "small table with keys, mail". |
| 6 | **Mom's birthday card** | On the side table with the mail. Unopened mail by the door is unopened life. You pass it every time you come home. |

## Decisions (not places)

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Task order: toast → bath → clean-up → TV → phone → cat** | GDD Phase 1 order (`STORY_FLOW.md` steps 2–7). The call comes after TV (GDD: "rings once before the sleep trigger"), which gives the interruption its sting. |
| **Moving stands you up from the couch** | No extra button to learn. The moment you want to leave, you can. |
| **The TV's +10 cap covers everything the TV does** (channels, batteries, ghost grab) | Appendix 1: "TV can add at most +10 in total". Calming (−10) is never capped. |
| **Ghost grab: 10% per press, only above 50%** | GDD: "low chance, frustration > 50%". Ten percent keeps it a surprise, not a routine. Editable in `TvTuning`. |
| **The cat waits 20 s before leaving** | The GDD doesn't say. It's long enough to notice it and walk over, and short enough that ignoring it is a real choice. Editable in `CatTuning`. |
| **The cat arrives after 1.5 s of you standing still** | GDD: "while the player is sitting or standing still". After the call you're standing at the shelf, so it comes down the corridor towards you. |
| **The demo videos fix the dice** | So every outcome shows up in 60 seconds. The real odds are covered by the tests. |

## How this is checked

The Play Mode tests check the reasons that can be measured:
- you can't see the phone from the couch, and you can walk to it
- from the phone you have a clear view to where the cat appears
- the couch faces the TV, and nothing blocks the screen
- the Locked Room can't be entered
- clutter never hides behind furniture (the builder needs open floor around each item)
